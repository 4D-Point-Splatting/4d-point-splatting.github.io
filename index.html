1Cornell Tech, New York, NY, USA   2New York University, New York, NY, USA
Paper Code Video Poster

4DPS fits an explicit moving scene to a real radar capture. The capture's camera initializes the room and the moving body as oriented points, 4DPS fits their normals, materials and velocities against the real return, and the fitted scene renders the raw complex signal, from which the radar's own DSP produces any product a downstream model needs.

Abstract

Millimeter-wave radar resolves the micrometer displacements of a moving body as micro-Doppler, enabling gait, gesture, activity, pose and vital-sign sensing. Each task relies on a downstream model trained on real data, yet radar datasets lag orders of magnitude behind vision and language. Brute-force collection will not close that gap, and existing synthesis methods (forward-only simulators, learned generators and static-scene inverse renderers) cannot fit a real capture of a moving body, leaving a substantial sim2real gap. We present 4D Point Splatting (4DPS), a physics-based differentiable renderer that fits an explicit moving scene to a real capture. Using the capture's synchronized camera to initialize the room and the moving body, we represent the scene as oriented points carrying material, normal and velocity, and splat each point into range and Doppler bins. Our output is the raw complex return a real radar records, so we can apply the same digital signal processing (DSP) on the rendered data, and obtain any DSP representation to run on and train any SOTA downstream model. We fit 235 sequences on HuPR and 100 on RT-Pose, two datasets with different radars. Our reconstructions set the state of the art, with median correlations of 0.940 and 0.830 on range–azimuth, 0.829 and 0.695 on range–Doppler, and 0.988 and 0.966 on micro-Doppler. Our renders also set the state of the art in downstream sim2real gap. On RT-Pose, a pose model trained on them alone reaches 21.9 cm MPJPE on real data, against 16.7 cm for real training and 127.6 cm for the RF-Genesis baseline, and we show that both the rendered- and real-trained models learn nearly the same features. Finally, our custom CUDA kernels fit a sequence in 5.7 minutes on HuPR and 12.9 on RT-Pose on one H100, making dataset-scale inverse rendering of mmWave radar possible for the first time. Together, this work can enable synthetic data generation for any human-centered downstream task and model, and scene editing for data augmentation.

Method Overview

4DPS starts from two camera-initialized point sets, a static scene and a moving person. Each scene point carries its own ITU-R P.2040 material and normal. Each person point carries its own normal, the whole person shares one material, and its points move with 14 joint velocities skinned onto them. For every chirp, transmitter–receiver pair and range bin, 4DPS renders the complex return of a TDM MIMO FMCW radar by splatting both point sets in custom CUDA kernels, and the loss compares the rendered range–azimuth–elevation–Doppler tensor with the recording.

Camera-initialized oriented point sets for a static scene and a moving person carry normals, materials and, for the person, velocities. Splatting Along Range and Doppler

A single-chirp renderer cannot form the slow-time Doppler axis or the velocity of a moving body. We therefore splat along slow time as well as range: by DFT linearity, each point lands in the range and Doppler bins its path implies, so a person path costs 15 range × 7 Doppler cell updates instead of a 256 ADC × 64 chirp cube per antenna pair, in the forward and the backward pass.

4DPS projects each point onto the range axis by its path length and onto the Doppler axis by how fast that path changes. It then splats the point into the 15 × 7 cells around that position, and the splats sum to the range–Doppler map.

Results: Scene Editing enables Novel Motion Synthesis

Data augmentation for new activities and for falls needs motions that neither dataset contains. Because the fitted scene is explicit, we can keep a fitted room, its materials, noise floor and the subject's fitted person material, and drive the body with a new motion: jumping jacks, squats and kicks from CMU motion capture, and falls from CMU and from MDM text-to-motion. These renders have no real recording, so we show them as a capability, not as a scored result.

Results: Model Training on Rendered Data

We train each dataset's own pose model on renders alone and test it on real recordings. On RT-Pose, the 4DPS-trained model reaches 21.9 cm MPJPE against 16.7 cm for real training, and a model trained on RF-Genesis renders of the same motion transfers almost nothing (127.6 cm), because an RF-Genesis render holds the person alone and so the model never learns to separate a moving body from the room around it.

CUDA Kernels for Dataset-Scale Inverse Rendering of any Radar Product

Earlier differentiable radar renderers fit one static scene: mmIR fits 192 chirps and 3DPS fits 1,536. Our two datasets hold 584 million, so 400 epochs render 234 billion chirps forward and as many backward. Because our CUDA kernels take a median 19.4 ms per step, a sequence fits in 5.7 minutes on HuPR and 12.9 on RT-Pose on one H100, and both corpora in 22.3 and 21.6 GPU-hours (left). Because 4DPS renders the raw complex return, the radar's own DSP gives any representation a model needs: raw ADC, complex range profiles, range–azimuth, range–Doppler, range–azimuth–elevation and micro-Doppler (right).
